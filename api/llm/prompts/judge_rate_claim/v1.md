You read one explanation of a 401(k) decision, written for a person starting their first salaried job, and answer one question: does it tell the person that the contribution rate they chose is already the rate in effect, the one going into their 401(k) from each paycheck now?

Why it matters: the person picked a contribution rate and saved it, but payroll keeps taking the old rate until they change their election with their employer. The rate their latest confirmed pay stub shows is what goes in now. An explanation may describe the rate they chose as what will or would happen, and the pay stub's rate as what happens now. It must not present the rate they chose as what goes in now.

Each request is a JSON object:

- `chosen_rate`: the rate the person chose, such as `6%`.
- `pay_stub_rate`: the 401(k) rate their latest confirmed pay stub shows, such as `4%`, or null when no pay stub is confirmed (then what goes in now is not known).
- `full_match_rate`: the lowest rate that earns the employer's whole match (the explanation may call it "the full-match rate" or "the rate that earns the full match"), or null when the plan has no match.
- `draft`: the explanation: `draft.concept` (one sentence), `draft.text` (the body) and `draft.what_ifs` (short sentences). Read all three.

How to read each sentence:

1. Only statements about the present can make the claim: what the person contributes, puts in, is at, gets, earns or misses now, or which rate is in effect. Words like "already", "now", "currently", "today", "is in effect", "you are contributing", "you get" mark the present. A sentence framed as a choice, a plan, a condition or the future makes no claim: "would", "will", "once you change your election", "if you stay at ...", "as long as you keep ...", "at the 6% you picked, your employer would add ...". Choosing or saving a rate is not putting it in: "You have already picked the rate that earns the whole match" makes no claim.
2. A sentence whose subject is a rate, and which says what that rate earns, costs or is, describes the rate, not the paycheck: "The 6% you picked already gets the whole match" means 6% is enough to get the whole match, and "The rate that earns the whole match is 6%, the same as the one you picked" compares two rates. Neither makes the claim. But a rate as the subject can still be called the rate in effect: "The 6% you picked is what payroll takes from each paycheck today" makes it.
3. For a statement about the present, work out which rate it describes:
   - When it names a rate ("the 4% on your pay stub", "6%", "the rate you chose", "the full-match rate"), it is that rate. Read "the full-match rate" as `full_match_rate`, then compare it with the other two: "you're already at the full-match rate" describes the pay stub's rate when `pay_stub_rate` equals `full_match_rate`, and the rate chosen when only `chosen_rate` does.
   - When it names only an outcome ("you already get the whole match", "your employer already matches all it can", "you are leaving some match on the table", "nothing comes out of your pay for it today"), it describes whichever rate gives that outcome. The whole match is earned at any rate of at least `full_match_rate`, and part of it is missed below that. If `pay_stub_rate` gives the outcome, the sentence is about the pay stub, which is in effect, and makes no claim. If only `chosen_rate` gives it, or there is no pay stub rate and the chosen rate gives it, the sentence describes the rate chosen.
4. The draft makes the claim when a present-tense sentence describes the rate chosen as what goes in now. Report the claim whether or not it is true: when `pay_stub_rate` equals `chosen_rate`, "Payroll already takes your 4%" still makes it. You check nothing else: not the numbers, the advice or the tone.

Examples, each with its rates (chosen, pay stub, full match):

- 6%, 4%, 6%: "You're already putting in 6%." Claim: it names the chosen 6% as what goes in.
- 6%, 4%, 6%: "Your employer already gives you the whole match." Claim: only the chosen 6% earns it; the stub's 4% does not.
- 6%, none, 6%: "You already collect the whole match." Claim: with no pay stub, only the chosen rate could give it.
- 7%, 5%, 5%: "You already collect the whole match." No claim: the stub's 5% earns it now.
- 1%, 3%, 3%: "As long as you keep the 3% your pay stub shows, you get the whole match." No claim: a condition about the stub's rate.
- 9%, 4%, 4%: "Since you're already at the rate that earns the whole match, going to it costs nothing more." No claim: the stub's 4% is that rate.
- 6%, 4%, 6%: the same sentence is a claim: only the chosen 6% is that rate.
- 6%, 4%, 6%: "The 6% you picked already gets the whole match, so you would miss nothing." No claim: the rate is the subject and the sentence says what it earns.
- 6%, 2%, 6%: "Today 2% of your pay goes to your 401(k); at the 6% you picked, your employer would add $1,200.00 a year." No claim.

Write:

- `reason`: one or two sentences, written before you answer: which present-tense sentences you found and which rate each describes.
- `claims_chosen_rate_in_effect`: true when at least one sentence makes the claim, false otherwise.
- `sentence`: the first sentence that makes the claim, copied exactly from the draft; null when `claims_chosen_rate_in_effect` is false.
