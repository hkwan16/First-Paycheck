You read one explanation of a Form W-4 decision, written for a person starting their first salaried job, and answer one question: does it tell the person that the federal income tax their new Form W-4 entries would withhold, or the refund or balance due those entries lead to, is already what happens with their pay now?

Why it matters: the person filled in Form W-4 entries and saved them, but payroll keeps withholding what it does now until they give their employer the new form. What their latest confirmed pay stub withholds is what is taken from each paycheck now, and the refund or balance due at that withholding is what the year comes to if nothing changes. An explanation may describe the entries' withholding and their projection as what would or will happen, and the pay stub's withholding as what happens now. It must not present the entries' withholding or their projection as what happens now.

Each request is a JSON object:

- `entries_withholding`: the federal income tax the entries would withhold from each paycheck, such as `$512.40`.
- `pay_stub_withholding`: what the latest confirmed pay stub withholds from each paycheck, such as `$380.00`, or null when no pay stub is known (then what is withheld now is not known).
- `entries_outcome`: the projection with the entries, in words, such as `a refund of $1.20`, `a balance due of $40.00` or `no refund and nothing due`.
- `pay_stub_outcome`: the projection if every paycheck of the year withholds what the pay stub does, in the same words, or null with no pay stub.
- `draft`: the explanation: `draft.concept` (one sentence), `draft.text` (the body) and `draft.what_ifs` (short sentences). Read all three.

How to read each sentence:

1. Only statements about the present can make the claim: what the employer or payroll withholds, takes or keeps now, what the person is on track for, or what their withholding covers now. Words like "already", "now", "currently", "today", "right now", "is withheld", "your employer withholds", "you are on track" mark the present. A sentence framed as a choice, a plan, a condition or the future makes no claim: "would", "will", "once your employer has the new form", "if you hand in these entries", "with these entries, ...". Choosing or saving entries is not withholding them: "You have already picked entries that would cover your tax" makes no claim.
2. When the entries (or the amount they give) are the subject, and the sentence says what they withhold or lead to, it describes the entries, not the paycheck: "These entries withhold $512.40 a paycheck" and "The $512.40 these entries give comes close to the year's tax" make no claim. A sentence that sets the two amounts side by side ("these entries give $512.40, against the $380.00 on your pay stub") compares them and makes no claim.
3. For a statement about the present, work out which withholding it describes:
   - When it names an amount, it is the one with that amount: `entries_withholding` or `pay_stub_withholding`. When it names the pay stub ("your pay stub withholds ..."), it is the pay stub's.
   - When it names only an outcome ("you are on track for a refund of about $1.20", "your withholding almost exactly covers your tax", "you will owe at tax time"), it describes whichever projection gives that outcome. Compare it with `entries_outcome` and `pay_stub_outcome`. If the pay stub's outcome fits, the sentence is about the pay stub, which is what happens now, and makes no claim. If only the entries' outcome fits, or there is no pay stub and the entries' outcome fits, the sentence describes the entries.
4. The draft makes the claim when a present-tense sentence describes the entries' withholding or their outcome as what happens now. Report the claim whether or not it is true: when `pay_stub_withholding` equals `entries_withholding`, "Your employer withholds $512.40 from each paycheck" still makes it. You check nothing else: not the numbers, the advice or the tone.

Examples, each with its figures (entries, pay stub; entries' outcome, pay stub's outcome):

- $512.40, $380.00; a refund of $1.20, a balance due of $3,432.00: "Payroll takes $512.40 out of every paycheck." Claim: it names the entries' amount as what is withheld now.
- $512.40, $380.00; a refund of $1.20, a balance due of $3,432.00: "Your withholding lines up with your tax almost to the cent." Claim: only the entries' outcome is close to even.
- $512.40, none; a refund of $1.20, none: "You are set to get a small refund." Claim: with no pay stub, only the entries could give it.
- $512.40, $380.00; a refund of $1.20, a balance due of $3,432.00: "As things stand you are headed for a bill of about $3,432 at tax time." No claim: that is the pay stub's outcome.
- $512.40, $380.00; a refund of $1.20, a balance due of $3,432.00: "Each paycheck now has $380.00 taken out for federal income tax; with the new entries it would be $512.40." No claim.
- $512.40, $380.00; a refund of $1.20, a balance due of $3,432.00: "These entries withhold $512.40 a paycheck, which nearly matches the year's tax." No claim: the entries are the subject.
- $512.40, none; a refund of $1.20, none: "If your employer uses these entries, the year would end with a refund of about $1.20." No claim.

Write:

- `reason`: one or two sentences, written before you answer: which present-tense sentences you found and which withholding each describes.
- `claims_entries_in_effect`: true when at least one sentence makes the claim, false otherwise.
- `sentence`: the first sentence that makes the claim, copied exactly from the draft; null when `claims_entries_in_effect` is false.
