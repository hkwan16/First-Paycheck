# Before and after quiz

The same five questions are given twice: before the participant sees the app, and again after the
tasks. Hand out a fresh copy each time and do not show the first answers. Give no hints and do not
say whether an answer is right until both rounds are done. About 5 minutes per round.

Each question is one point; "I do not know" scores zero. The target in the technical spec is an
average gain of at least 2 points.

---

## Questions (participant copy)

Participant number: ______ Round: Before / After

For each question, circle one answer. Then circle how sure you are, from 1 (guessing) to 5
(certain).

**1. 401(k) match.** Your employer matches 100% of what you put into your 401(k), up to 4% of your
salary. You earn $65,000 a year and put in 2% of your salary. How much does your employer put in
over the year?

- a. $0, because you did not put in 4%
- b. $1,300
- c. $2,600
- d. $3,900
- e. I do not know

How sure: 1 2 3 4 5

**2. Pre-tax contributions.** You start putting $100 from each paycheck into a traditional
(pre-tax) 401(k). What happens to your take-home pay?

- a. It goes down by exactly $100
- b. It goes down by less than $100, because federal income tax is figured on the lower pay
- c. It goes down by more than $100, because of a 401(k) fee
- d. It does not change until you file your tax return
- e. I do not know

How sure: 1 2 3 4 5

**3. Form W-4, Step 4(c).** What does a dollar amount in Step 4(c) ("extra withholding") of Form
W-4 do?

- a. Lowers the tax you owe for the year by that amount
- b. Has that extra amount of federal income tax withheld from each paycheck
- c. Sends that amount from each paycheck to a savings account
- d. Tells your employer how much you expect to get back as a refund
- e. I do not know

How sure: 1 2 3 4 5

**4. Emergency fund.** How is an emergency fund target usually set?

- a. A fixed $1,000, whatever you spend
- b. 10% of your yearly salary
- c. A number of months of your essential expenses, often 3 to 6
- d. Whatever is left in checking at the end of the month
- e. I do not know

How sure: 1 2 3 4 5

**5. High-deductible health plan (HDHP).** Which is true of an HDHP compared with a traditional
plan?

- a. Higher premiums and a lower deductible
- b. Lower premiums, a higher deductible, and it can be paired with a health savings account (HSA)
- c. It covers only emergencies
- d. It has no deductible but higher copays
- e. I do not know

How sure: 1 2 3 4 5

---

## Answer key (facilitator only)

| Q   | Answer | Why                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| --- | ------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | b      | The match is 100% of what you put in, up to 4%: 2% of $65,000 is $1,300. Putting in 4% would earn $2,600, so $1,300 of match is left behind. The app's 401(k) card shows exactly this. |
| 2   | b      | A traditional 401(k) deferral comes out before federal income tax is figured, so the tax withheld drops too. Social Security and Medicare are still taken on it.                       |
| 3   | b      | Step 4(c) is an extra amount withheld from each paycheck, on top of what the rest of the form works out. It does not change the tax owed for the year.                                 |
| 4   | c      | Targets are counted in months of essential spending; the app's calculator starts at 3 months and goes up to 12.                                                                        |
| 5   | b      | An HDHP trades lower premiums for a higher deductible, and qualifying HDHPs can be paired with an HSA, which the health plan card's notice explains.                                   |
