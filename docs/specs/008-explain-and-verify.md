# 008: Explain, verify, citations and retrieval

Status: Implemented, M3. Rule 7a, `explain/v2`, `fallback/v2` and the `rate_framing` eval metric added for issue 132: the explanation tells the rate the person chose from the rate their pay stub shows. Rules 24a and 24b: `rate_framing` is scored by an LLM judge, calibrated by `judge_accuracy`. Rule 19a and migration `0019_explanation_context_digest` added for issue 161: an explanation written from facts that have changed since reads as out of date and can be written again. Rule 7b, `explain/v3`, `fallback/v4`, the `explain-w4` eval suite and its `withholding_framing` metric (rules 24c to 24e) added for issue 181: the W-4 explanation tells the federal income tax the newest pay stub withholds from what the saved Form W-4 entries would withhold, and states the refund or balance due at the stub's withholding. The new prompt and fallback versions make every explanation stored before them read as out of date under rule 19a, 401(k) and health plan ones included; that is intended, and each is written again once, when the person asks.

## Goal

Explain a saved decision in plain words from the calculator's own numbers, the curated IRS passages for that calculator and the user's benefits text, and show the explanation only after plain code has checked that every number in it is one the calculator produced or the user confirmed and every citation is a curated passage. A draft that fails gets one retry with the failure attached; a second failure shows the plain calculator result, flagged as a fallback. Code: `api/retrieval/` (citations, benefits search, the explain context), `api/verify/` (number extraction, checks, the draft-verify-retry-fallback flow and the fallback text), `api/llm/prompts/explain/v3.md` (v1 and v2 are kept for the rows they wrote), `ExplanationDraft` in `api/llm/schemas.py`, `Gateway.explain`, `Gateway.judge_rate_claim` and `api/llm/prompts/judge_rate_claim/v1.md` (the explain eval's judge, rule 24a), `Gateway.judge_withholding_claim` and `api/llm/prompts/judge_withholding_claim/v1.md` (the `explain-w4` eval's judge, rule 24d), routes `POST /explain` and `GET /decisions/{id}/explanation`, the seed `api/db/seed/irs_citations/k401.yaml`, and the eval suites `explain`, `explain-w4` and `explain-smoke`.

## Inputs

- `POST /explain`: `decision_id` (uuid) and `force` (bool, default false).
- `GET /decisions/{id}/explanation`: the decision id.
- The decision's `inputs` and `result` (spec 007 rule 3), the `irs_citations` rows for its calculator and `tax_year`, and up to three of the user's `benefits_chunks` that match the calculator's search query.
- The user's newest confirmed pay stub's `retirement_contribution_pct` (spec 005 rule 24b), or null when no stub is confirmed (rule 7a). It is read when the explanation runs and never stored on the decision.
- The same stub's `federal_withholding_cents` when the stub is on the decision's pay schedule, or null (rule 7b). It is read when the explanation runs and never stored on the decision.

## Outputs

`ExplanationOut`: `id`, `decision_id`, `concept`, `text`, `numbers` (`NumberClaim`: `value`, `label`), `what_ifs` (strings), `citations` (`CitationOut`: `key`, `title`, `url`, `passage`, `source`), `verified`, `fallback`, `attempt`, `retries`, `prompt_version`, `verification` (`numbers_checked`, `numbers_unmatched`, `citations_checked`, `citations_unknown`, `has_citation`), `stale` (rule 19a) and `created_at`. `POST /explain` and `GET /decisions/{id}/explanation` both return it.

## Rules

### Citations and retrieval

1. **Seed.** `api/db/seed/irs_citations/<calculator prefix>.yaml` lists entries with `key`, `calculator`, `tax_year`, `title`, `url` (https, irs.gov), `source` (the IRS document name), `passage`, `verified` and `verified_on`. A passage is quoted exactly from the live page, under 80 words, with `...` marking any omitted words; each part between ellipses appears verbatim in the page text. An entry whose page could not be fetched says `verified: false` and is not loaded.
2. **Keys for 2026** (`k401.yaml`): `k401.deferral_limit` (Notice 2025-67, 402(g)), `k401.catch_up` (IR-2025-111), `k401.compensation_limit` (Notice 2025-67, 401(a)(17)), `k401.employer_match` (IRS, matching contributions), `k401.pretax_deferral` (IRS 401(k) Resource Guide), `k401.vesting` (IRS, Retirement topics: Vesting), `k401.federal_brackets` (IR-2025-103). They cover every key spec 006 rule 11 can emit.
3. **Table.** `irs_citations` has no `user_id`: `calculator calculator_kind`, `key`, `title`, `url` (`CHECK (url LIKE 'https://%')`), `passage`, `source`, `tax_year smallint`, `verified_on date`, `created_at`, `updated_at`; `UNIQUE (calculator, key, tax_year)`. RLS is enabled and forced, `authenticated` holds `SELECT` with a `USING (true)` policy, and nothing else may write; the seed loader writes on the privileged connection.
4. **Loader.** `make seed` upserts every verified entry on `(calculator, key, tax_year)` and deletes rows the files no longer list, in one transaction; running it twice changes nothing.
5. **`retrieval/citations.py:for_calculator(session, calculator, tax_year)`** returns that calculator's citations for the year, ordered by key.
6. **`retrieval/benefits_search.py:search(session, user_id, query, k)`** ranks the user's `benefits_chunks` against the stored `search` vector with one `plainto_tsquery('english', word)` per whitespace-separated word of `query`, joined with `||` (OR), so user words are never parsed as tsquery syntax and a chunk needs only one word to match (a single `plainto_tsquery` of the whole query would AND every word, and real plan text rarely holds all of them in one chunk), ordered by `ts_rank` descending then `(document_id, chunk_index)`, and returns at most `k` (`text`, `rank`, `document_id`, `chunk_index`). It runs under RLS and also filters on `user_id`. The explain context uses `k = 3` and the query `401(k) retirement match vesting` for `k401_match`.

### The context and the prompt

7. **Facts.** `retrieval/context.py` turns a decision into `ExplainContext`: the calculator, `tax_year`, a list of facts (`label`, `value`) with every money value written as dollars with cents (`$2,400.00`) and every percentage as `5%` or `4.5%`, the allowed citations (`key`, `title`, `passage`) and the benefits passages. Only redacted text is ever in `benefits_chunks` (spec 004), so nothing unredacted reaches the model.
7a. **The rate the pay stub shows (`k401_match`, issue 132).** A decision's `contribution_pct` is the rate the person chose; payroll keeps taking the old rate until they change their election with their employer, so it is not what they contribute now. `POST /explain` reads, in its first session, the newest confirmed pay stub (`pay_date DESC, created_at DESC`, as `GET /pay-stubs` lists them) and passes its `retirement_contribution_pct` (spec 005 rule 24b) on as `pay_stub_pct`, null without a stub; calculators other than `k401_match` ignore it. It goes into the explanation only, never into the decision: decisions are immutable (spec 007) and a stub confirmed after the save can show a different rate, so the same decision explained after a new stub may be explained differently, and each run's rows keep what was written then. In the context: the first fact is `Contribution rate you chose` (the decision's rate) and, when a stub rate is known, the second is `401(k) contribution rate your latest pay stub shows` (formatted like every percentage, `3%`); the facts about the match and the yearly contribution say "at the rate you chose". Which case holds is decided once, by `calc/pay_stub.py:stub_rate(chosen, pay_stub_pct)`, comparing the two rates by value (`3.00` equals `3`): `StubRate.CHOSEN` (the stub shows the chosen rate: it is in effect now), `StubRate.OTHER` (the stub shows another rate: that is what goes in now, and the chosen rate takes effect once the election is changed with the employer) or `StubRate.NONE` (no pay stub is confirmed: what goes in now is not known). The context's one note (no digits), the fallback's stub sentence and its full-match sentence (rule 17) and the eval's `rate_framing` (rule 24) are each looked up by that value. `retrieval/context.py:explain_setup` builds the context, the allowed numbers, the citation keys and the fallback (rule 17) from the same `pay_stub_pct`, for the route and the eval suites alike.
7b. **The withholding the pay stub shows (`w4`, issue 181).** A W-4 decision's `withholding_per_paycheck_cents` and `projected_refund_cents` are what the Form W-4 entries the person saved would withhold and lead to; payroll keeps withholding what it does now until the person gives the employer the new form, so they are not what is withheld now. The e2e account's explanation under `explain/v2` said "Your employer withholds $423.16 of federal income tax from each paycheck ... withholding is almost exactly matched" while its pay stub withheld $272.44 and the W-4 card said $3,918.70 would be due at tax time (spec 014 example 11): the facts labeled the entries' figure `Federal income tax withheld per paycheck` and gave no stub figure at all. Rule 7a's pattern applies:
    - **Read at explain time.** `POST /explain` reads, in its first session, the same newest confirmed pay stub as rule 7a (one read serves both) and passes its `federal_withholding_cents` on as `pay_stub_withholding_cents` when the stub is on the decision's pay schedule, else null: `calc/pay_stub.py:withholding_on_schedule(federal_withholding_cents, pay_frequency, pay_periods_per_year)` is null when the stub's `pay_frequency` is set and its pay periods a year (`calc.pay_dates.PERIODS`) differ from the decision's `pay_periods_per_year`, the test the W-4 card applies before it compares (spec 014 rule 10a, "No stub, or a zero"). Calculators other than `w4` ignore it. It never goes into the decision. A W-4 decision may carry its own `current_withholding_per_paycheck_cents` (spec 014 rule 17a, saved after issue 153) or not (saved before it; decisions are immutable); the explanation states neither, because the stub is what payroll does now and a stub confirmed after the save (the first one after the employer applies the form, for example) is newer than anything the decision holds. Like the rate, a newer stub with another withholding changes the context, so a stored explanation reads as out of date (rule 19a).
    - **The case** is decided once, by `calc/pay_stub.py:stub_withholding(entries_cents, pay_stub_withholding_cents)`, comparing whole cents: `StubWithholding.ENTRIES` (the stub withholds what the entries give: they are in effect now), `OTHER` (the stub withholds another amount: that is what is withheld now, and the entries take effect once the employer has the new form) or `NONE` (no confirmed stub on the decision's pay schedule: what is withheld now is not known). The context's stub note, its outcome notes, the facts it adds, the fallback's sentences and the `explain-w4` eval's `withholding_framing` (rule 24c) are each looked up by that value. A stub within payroll's whole-dollar rounding of the entries (spec 014 rule 10a, "Rounding") is `OTHER`: the explanation states both amounts and both outcomes, each of which is true, and does not guess whether the difference is rounding.
    - **The figures at the stub** come from `calc/w4.py:at_withholding(entries, result, pay_stub_withholding_cents)`: `annual_withholding_cents = w0 * P` and `refund_cents = annual_withholding_cents + other_jobs_withholding_cents - projected_tax_after_credits_cents`, spec 014 rule 10a's arithmetic against the decision's stored tax. `w4()` computes its own rule 10a fields with the same function, so for the same stub the explanation and the W-4 card's "If you change nothing" agree.
    - **The context.** The withholding facts say whose they are: `Federal income tax these entries would withhold per paycheck`, then, when the stub's withholding is known, `Federal income tax your latest pay stub withholds per paycheck`, then `Federal income tax these entries would withhold per year`; for `OTHER` also `Federal income tax withheld per year if every paycheck withholds what your latest pay stub does` and, after `Projected refund with these entries` (or `Projected balance due with these entries`), `Projected refund at your pay stub's withholding` (or `Projected balance due at your pay stub's withholding`). The first note is the stub note for the case; the next says the entries' projection in words ("With these entries the projection is a refund ..."), and for `OTHER` one more says the outcome at the stub's withholding ("At what the latest pay stub withholds, the year ends in a balance due ..."). Notes hold no digits. The rest of the W-4 facts and notes are spec 014 rule 19's.
    - **Allowed numbers.** `retrieval/context.py:pay_stub_numbers` gives, for a `w4` decision with a known stub withholding, `pay_stub_withholding_per_paycheck_cents`, `pay_stub_annual_withholding_cents` and `pay_stub_refund_cents` (rule 12), so every figure the context states verifies.
    - **The fallback** (rule 17) says what the entries would withhold and the projection with them, then, by case: for `ENTRIES` that the latest stub withholds the same, so the entries are in effect now; for `OTHER` the stub's withholding now, the year's withholding and the refund or balance due if every paycheck withholds that, and that the entries change it once the person gives the employer the updated Form W-4; for `NONE` nothing about what is withheld now.
8. **Prompt** `llm/prompts/explain/v3.md` (model `explain`, schema `ExplanationDraft`) tells the model to use only the facts' numbers, written exactly as given or as whole dollars, to cite only the given keys and at least one, and to avoid dates, ages and any other figure. For a 401(k) decision it explains the rate the person chose, keeps it apart from the rate their pay stub shows (what goes in now), and describes the chosen rate with "would" or "will" unless a note says the stub shows it, never as something the person already contributes or gets. For a W-4 decision (rule 7b) it says what the entries would withhold and the projection with them, keeps that apart from what the latest pay stub withholds (what is withheld now) and the refund or balance due at it (what happens if nothing changes), and describes the entries' withholding and projection with "would" or "will" unless a note says the stub withholds what they give. Nothing in code rejects a draft for its wording: the verifier checks numbers and citations (rule 14), and how often drafts frame the rate correctly is measured by the eval (rule 24, `rate_framing`). It describes a match formula only by the tiers the facts give, never by a tier's width ("50% of the next 2%"), and writes no percentage from the benefits passages that the facts do not hold (a vesting schedule, "100% vested"): the live eval's remaining number failures were those. `explain/v1` told the model to "say what the person gets at their current contribution" with only the chosen rate in the facts, so it called a chosen rate the current one ("You are already contributing 5%" for a stub at 3%); it stays on disk for the rows it wrote. `explain/v2` (issue 132) had the 401(k) rules but no W-4 ones, so it read a W-4 decision's withholding as what is withheld now (rule 7b); it stays on disk too, and the gateway sends v3, which is v2's text plus the W-4 rules. A released prompt file is never edited.
9. **Draft schema.** `ExplanationDraft`: `concept` (one sentence naming the idea), `text` (two to five short paragraphs), `numbers` (list of `NumberClaim`: `value` as written in the text and a `label`), `what_ifs` (up to three short strings) and `citation_keys`.
10. **Gateway.** `Gateway.explain(user_id, context, feedback=None)` sends the context as JSON (and, on a retry, the verifier's feedback) and returns an `ExplanationDraft`, with the budget, refusal branch, validation and ledger every gateway call has. `FakeGateway` writes a draft from the facts and cites the first allowed key, so it verifies.

### Verification (no LLM)

11. **`verify/numbers.py:extract_numbers(text)`** finds every number in `text` and returns `NumberToken(raw, value, kind)` with `kind` in `money` (a `$`, with an optional leading `-`), `percent` (followed by `%` or the word `percent`) or `plain`. It reads `$1,234.56`, `1234`, `6%`, `6.0%`, `-$12`. It skips digits that belong to a word or a code reference: statute numbers followed by a parenthesis (`401(k)`, `402(g)`, `414(v)`) and their numbered subsections (`401(a)(17)`), form names (`W-4`, `W-2`, `1040`, preceded by "Form"), Form W-4 step numbers (`Step 2`, `Steps 3`, added for spec 014) and digits glued to letters (`3rd`, `k401`, `4(c)`).
12. **Allowed numbers** (`verify/allowed.py:AllowedNumbers.from_decision(inputs, result)`): every value in the decision's inputs and result, walked recursively. Keys with a `_cents` part (`salary_cents`, `employer_match_cents_full`) give money values, allowed both exactly (`$2,400.00`, `$2,400`) and as whole dollars rounded half up (`$46` for 4,615 cents). Keys with a `_pct` or `_rate` part (`contribution_pct`, `employee_pct_upto`, `marginal_rate_used`) give percentages. `tax_year` is an allowed year; `pay_periods_per_year` is an allowed count; any other integer input (`age`) is an allowed plain number. Booleans and strings are skipped. The pay stub's rate (rule 7a) is allowed too: `retrieval/context.py:pay_stub_numbers` gives `{"pay_stub_contribution_pct": "3.00"}` for a `k401_match` decision with a stub rate, which `from_decision` reads as a percentage like any `_pct` key; for a `w4` decision with a stub withholding it gives the stub's withholding per paycheck and the year's withholding and refund or balance due at it (rule 7b), read as money by their `_cents` keys; nothing otherwise.
13. **Matching.** A money token matches when its magnitude equals an allowed money value exactly, or, written without cents, equals an allowed whole-dollar value. A percent token matches an allowed percentage by value (`6%`, `6.0%` and `6.00%` are the same). A plain token matches an allowed year, count or plain number, or a money value in dollars (`2,400` for 240,000 cents). The sign of a money token is ignored: `-$12` reads as a drop of $12.
14. **`verify/verify.py:verify_explanation(draft, allowed_numbers, allowed_citation_keys)`** extracts numbers from `concept`, `text`, every `what_ifs` entry and every `NumberClaim` value, and returns `Verification`: `numbers_checked`, `numbers_unmatched` (the raw tokens that matched nothing), `citations_checked`, `citations_unknown` (keys not allowed), `has_citation` and `passed` (no unmatched number, no unknown key, at least one citation). `feedback()` describes each failure in one line for the retry.

### The flow (`verify/flow.py`) and storage

15. **Draft, verify, retry once, fall back.** Attempt 1 drafts and verifies. If it fails, attempt 2 drafts again with the feedback appended and verifies. If that fails too, a fallback row follows. A gateway error (`LLMError`: budget, refusal, schema, unavailable) ends the run at once with the fallback; nothing is retried after a refusal or a spent budget.
16. **Every attempt is a row.** Each drafted attempt writes one `explanations` row (`attempt` 1 or 2, `verified` from the check, `fallback = false`); the fallback writes one more row (`attempt` = the next number, `verified = false`, `fallback = true`). `retries` on every row is the number of retries made in the run so far (0 or 1). `prompt_version` is the explain prompt's (`explain/v3`) on drafted rows and `fallback/v4` (`verify/fallback.py:FALLBACK_VERSION`) on the fallback row, whose text no prompt wrote. `fallback/v1` wrote "You already earn the full match" at any chosen rate; v2 is rule 17's text; v3 writes a negative health plan cost as an amount ahead (spec 012 rule 17); v4 writes the W-4 text of rule 7b.
17. **Fallback text** (`verify/fallback.py`) is the plain calculator result written by code, for `k401_match`: the rate chosen and, when the pay stub's rate is known, either that the stub shows the same rate or the stub's rate and that the chosen rate takes effect once the election is changed with the employer; "You already earn the full match" only when the stub shows the chosen rate; the full-match percentage, the match at the chosen and full-match percentage, the dollars missed, the per-paycheck cost before and after federal tax with the marginal rate, the deferral limit and, when they apply, the capped and compensation-capped notes. For `w4`, the text of rule 7b and spec 014 rule 19. Its citations are the result's `citation_keys` that the seed holds, so a fallback is cited too.
18. **Table `explanations`.** `decision_id` (composite same-user foreign key, `ON DELETE CASCADE`) or `finding_id` (no foreign key until `0005`), `CHECK (num_nonnulls(decision_id, finding_id) = 1)`; `attempt smallint CHECK (attempt BETWEEN 1 AND 3)`; `retries smallint CHECK (retries IN (0, 1))`; `concept`, `text` (`NOT NULL`); `numbers`, `what_ifs`, `citations` (`jsonb` arrays, default `[]`; `citations` holds keys); `prompt_version`; `verified`, `fallback` with `CHECK (NOT (verified AND fallback))`; `verification jsonb` (the counts of rule 14, an object); `context_digest text` (rule 19a, `CHECK (context_digest ~ '^[0-9a-f]{64}$')`, null only on rows written before `0019`); `created_at timestamptz DEFAULT clock_timestamp()`, so rows written in one transaction keep their order. Append-only: the owner policy with `SELECT` and `INSERT` grants. Index `(decision_id, created_at)`.
19. **Latest.** The latest explanation of a decision is its row with the greatest `(created_at, attempt)`.
19a. **Out of date (issue 161).** An explanation is checked against the facts it was written from, so it is only as good as those facts are now. A decision never changes (spec 007), but what it is explained from can: a newer confirmed pay stub with another 401(k) rate (rule 7a) or another federal withholding (rule 7b), a benefits summary confirmed or replaced (the passages, rule 6), an edited seed passage (rule 4), code that writes a fact or note differently, or a new prompt or fallback version. The explanation written before issue 132's fix said "You are already contributing 5%" beside a stub at 3%, and it stayed the verified answer forever.
    - **The digest.** Every row of a run stores `context_digest`, the SHA-256 (lowercase hex) of the run's `ExplainContext` (rule 7) as canonical JSON: `retrieval/context.py:context_digest`, `model_dump(mode="json")` written with sorted keys, no whitespace and UTF-8. The context holds every fact, note, citation passage and benefits passage the drafts were written from and the verifier's allowed numbers and keys follow from (rule 12: the decision's own inputs and result, plus the stub's rate or withholding and the figures at it, which are facts), so equal digests mean the same facts.
    - **Stale.** A row is stale when the context the decision would be explained from now (`app/routers/explain.py:current_setup`, the same reads as a run: the citations, the benefits passages, the newest stub's rate and withholding) has another digest, when the row has no digest, or when its `prompt_version` is not the one a run writes now (`explain/v3` on a draft, `FALLBACK_VERSION` on a fallback). A row with no digest was written before `0019`; what it was written from is unknown, so it is stale. That includes rows written after issue 132's fix, which costs each of them one rewrite, asked for by the person.
    - **Reads.** `GET /decisions/{id}/explanation` returns the latest row (rule 19) with `stale`, and `GET /plan` reports it on `PlanExplanation` (spec 016 rule 4). Both rebuild the current context with reads only, no model; a stale row is still returned as it was written, so the person sees what they read before and is told it is out of date.
    - **Why a digest, not something else.** Storing the stub's rate on the row and comparing it names one cause and misses the others; each new input would need its own column and its own comparison. Storing the whole context instead compares the same way but copies the passages and the redacted benefits text into every row (kilobytes) only to compare them; the digest is 64 characters and the context is rebuilt cheaply. Marking explanations stale when a stub or summary is confirmed needs every writer of every input to know about explanations, and rows are append-only (no `UPDATE` grant). Comparing `created_at` with the newest stub's would flag a new stub at the same rate and miss every other cause. Comparing digests at read time catches any change to what the model would be given, without the code knowing why it changed.
    - **No transaction across the model call, decisions immutable.** The digest is computed from the setup the first session read (rule 21) and written with the rows in the second; a stub confirmed during the model call leaves the new rows stale on the next read, which is true. Nothing is written to the decision.
19b. **Showing a stale row.** No surface presents a stale explanation as current. The Why page (`WhyScreen`) shows a stale verified row under a warning status, "Out of date", saying it was checked against figures that have changed since and may no longer be right, with "Write it again" (`POST /explain` without `force`, rule 20) above the old text; a stale fallback adds the same sentence to its status. The Plan page shows a stale verified row as "Explanation out of date. Write it again" (a warning, linking to Why) instead of the "Verified explanation" badge. The PDF labels it out of date and leaves its text and sources out (spec 016 rule 11), so a printed plan never carries a sentence that may be false. Confirming a pay stub or benefits summary reads the explanations again, so an open page learns it went stale.
20. **Idempotent `POST /explain`.** Without `force`, when the decision's latest verified row is current (not stale, rule 19a), it is returned and nothing is generated. Otherwise (none yet, only fallbacks, a stale latest verified row, or `force = true`) a new run starts. So the same facts never pay for a second run, and a client that shows a stale row can ask again without `force`: when another tab already wrote the current one, that row comes back.
21. **No transaction across the model call.** The route reads the decision and its context in one short user session, calls the gateway with no transaction open, and writes the run's rows in a second user session, so a 30-second model call never holds a pooled connection.
22. **Status codes.** `POST /explain` is 200 with the final row; another user's decision is 404; an LLM that is not configured (no key and `LLM_FAKE` off, which production refuses at startup, spec 003 rule 12a) is 503 `llm_unavailable`, raised only when a model call is needed: a 404 or a stored current verified explanation is answered without a model. `GET /decisions/{id}/explanation` is 404 `not_found` when the decision has no explanation. A user may start 30 runs per clock hour (UTC), counted with the upload limiter (`app/ratelimit.py`, bucket `explain.run`, spec 004 rule 5) in the first session, after the current context is read, the stored-explanation check and the model check: a 404, a stored current verified explanation and a 503 do not count, and the 31st run in a window is 429 `rate_limited` with `Retry-After` (seconds to the next window) and writes nothing.
22a. **Messages are for people.** Every error message is a sentence a person can read on its own (the web shows it, `apiErrorMessage`): no calculator, document type, field or code identifiers. A decision that cannot be explained (an emergency fund, spec 011) is 422 `explanation_unavailable` with "An emergency fund decision has no written explanation: no tax rule applies, so the calculator's result is the whole answer." `tests/unit/test_error_messages.py` refuses an error message with a `snake_case` or `SCREAMING_CASE` word, or one that interpolates an enum's `.value` or a `!r`. A validator's own `ValueError` is a sentence too and leaves as the whole message, without its field path (`app/errors.py:validation_message`): a person reaches the health plan rules ("A plan's deductible must not be more than its out-of-pocket maximum") by typing a plan on the health plan step or in a benefits summary, and the match tiers rule by typing tiers. Pydantic's own errors (a missing field, a wrong type) keep their field path; only a client's developer reads those. The web holds the same line on its side, for messages it did not choose (FastAPI's validation errors name fields, an older API): `apiErrorMessage` shows the caller's own sentence instead of a message with such a word (`web/src/lib/api/errors.ts:isPersonMessage`).
23. **Citations in the response** resolve the row's keys against `irs_citations` for the decision's calculator and `tax_year`, in the row's order.

## Evals

24. **`explain`** (LLM, `make eval-llm`): 29 synthetic decisions (`evals/fixtures/explain/decisions.json`, each a `k401_match` input whose result the suite computes with `calc/k401.py`, and the rate its pay stub shows, `pay_stub_contribution_pct`, or null for no stub; 10 since M3, 19 more for issue 132, with issue 132's stub-01 case and a stub rate other than the chosen rate in 14 of them, so that one flaky draft moves a metric by under 4 points rather than 10) through the real flow with `LLMGateway`, an in-memory ledger charged against `--max-cost-cents`, and the seed citations read from the YAML. Metrics: `first_try_verified` (share verified on attempt 1), `no_fallback` (1 minus the fallback rate), `cited` (share whose final row has at least one citation) and `rate_framing`: over the 18 decisions whose stub rate differs from the chosen rate or is null, the share whose final row (a draft or the fallback) does not claim the chosen rate is already in effect. `rate_framing` is scored by an LLM judge (rule 24a), called once per scored decision on its final row; a failure line names the decision and quotes the sentence the judge found. It measures drafts and never gates one: nothing in `POST /explain` calls the judge, and the verifier stays numbers and citations only (rule 14). `judge_accuracy` checks the judge itself: the share of the hand-labeled items in `evals/fixtures/explain/rate_claims.json` (rule 24b) on which its verdict matches the label, so a run shows whether a low `rate_framing` is the drafts or the judge misreading them. One case is one decision for the first three metrics (worth 1/29), one scored decision for `rate_framing` (1/18) and one labeled item for `judge_accuracy`. An earlier version scored `rate_framing` with two regular expressions (the person as the subject of "already", or the rate chosen called "already in effect"); they could not tell which rate a sentence was about, and flagged true sentences about the pay stub's rate ("If you stay at the 4% on your pay stub, you already get the full match") in live runs, so the judge replaced them. Baseline at the targets: `no_fallback >= 0.95` (technical spec: fallback rate at most 5%), `cited = 1.0`, `first_try_verified >= 0.8`; the recorded baseline is what the live runs measured (evals/CLAUDE.md, Rules), `rate_framing` and `judge_accuracy` included. Raises `SuiteUnavailable` without `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`.
24a. **The rate-claim judge.** `Gateway.judge_rate_claim(user_id, request)` sends a `RateClaimRequest` as JSON with `llm/prompts/judge_rate_claim/v1.md` (schema `RateClaimVerdict`) and returns the verdict, with the budget, refusal branch, validation and ledger every gateway call has. The request holds `chosen_rate` (the decision's `contribution_pct`, formatted like a fact, `5%`), `pay_stub_rate` (the stub's rate, or null for no confirmed pay stub), `full_match_rate` (the result's `full_match_pct`, or null when the plan has no match) and `draft`, the final row's `concept`, `text` and `what_ifs` (`RateClaimDraft`); nothing else of the decision. The full-match rate is there because the commonest wording names it instead of a number ("you are already at the full-match rate" is about the pay stub when the stub shows that rate, and a claim when only the chosen rate is it). The verdict is `reason` (one or two sentences on which rate the present-tense sentences describe, written first so the model works it out before it answers, and for reading misses), `claims_chosen_rate_in_effect` and `sentence`, the first sentence that makes the claim copied from the draft, required exactly when the claim is made (a reply that breaks this is a schema error). The judge reports the claim, not its truth: a draft that says the chosen rate is in effect when the stub shows that rate is a claim too, and `rate_framing` only counts the claim on the 18 decisions where it is false. A present-tense statement is a claim when it describes the rate chosen as what goes in now, by naming it or by naming an outcome ("you already earn the full match") that only the rate chosen gives; it is not one when it is about the stub's rate ("If you stay at the 4% on your pay stub, you already get the full match"), when a rate is its subject and it says what that rate earns ("The 5% you chose already earns the full match"), or when it is framed as a choice, a condition or the future. The prompt's front matter names the `explain` purpose, so the judge runs on `LLM_MODEL_EXPLAIN` and its ledger rows have purpose `explain` and prompt version `judge_rate_claim/v1`: a purpose of its own would need a new `llm_purpose` label (a migration) and a new `LLM_MODEL_*` setting for a call only the eval makes. A model grading its own kind of output can share its blind spots; `judge_accuracy` over items written by hand, many from live drafts, is the check on that. `FakeGateway` answers "no claim" (`claims_chosen_rate_in_effect` false, `sentence` null), so the free suites and the unit tests need no key; `ScriptedGateway` answers from its script like any call. The judge's errors (`LLMError`) do not stop the run: the item or decision counts as missed, with a failure line naming the error.
24b. **Calibration items** (`evals/fixtures/explain/rate_claims.json`): each has an `id`, the two rates and the full-match rate as a decision would give them (`chosen_pct`, `pay_stub_pct` or null, `full_match_pct` or null), the draft's `text` (and optionally `concept` and `what_ifs`), the label `claims` and, when it is a sentence from a live run, the fixture `decision` it came from (whose rates it must carry, a test checks). Synthetic only, at least 12 that make no claim and 8 that do, covering a rate as the subject, the person as the subject, conditionals, the stub's rate as what goes in now, no stub, a stub equal to the chosen rate, no match, and a claim in the concept or a what-if. Every row of the rate-framing table below is an item with the same rates and label (a test checks).
24c. **`explain-w4`** (LLM, `make eval-llm`): 12 synthetic W-4 decisions (`evals/fixtures/explain/w4_decisions.json`, each a `w4` input whose result the suite computes with `calc/w4.py`, and what its pay stub withholds each paycheck on the decision's pay schedule, `pay_stub_withholding_cents`, or null for none) through the same flow, ledger and seed citations as rule 24. Six have a stub that withholds another amount than the entries (issue 181's decision, spec 014 example 11, among them: $272.44 on the stub, $423.16 with the entries; one withholds more, one nothing, one within a dollar's rounding), three a stub that withholds what the entries give, three no stub. Metrics: `first_try_verified`, `no_fallback` and `cited` as in rule 24, and `withholding_framing`: over the 9 decisions whose case (rule 7b) is `OTHER` or `NONE`, the share whose final row does not claim the entries' withholding is already in effect, as the judge of rule 24d reads it; a failure line names the decision and quotes the judge's sentence. `judge_accuracy` is the share of the hand-labeled items of rule 24e the judge reads as labeled. One case is one decision (1/12), one scored decision for `withholding_framing` (1/9) and one item for `judge_accuracy`. It is a suite of its own, not more decisions in `explain`, so the 401(k) metrics and their measured baseline keep their meaning, and so the W-4 judge's calibration is not averaged into the rate judge's. Its recorded baseline is what the live runs measured (evals/CLAUDE.md, Rules). Raises `SuiteUnavailable` without `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`.
24d. **The withholding-claim judge.** `Gateway.judge_withholding_claim(user_id, request)` sends a `WithholdingClaimRequest` as JSON with `llm/prompts/judge_withholding_claim/v1.md` (schema `WithholdingClaimVerdict`) and returns the verdict, with the budget, refusal branch, validation and ledger every gateway call has. The request holds `entries_withholding` (the result's `withholding_per_paycheck_cents`, formatted like a fact, `$423.16`), `pay_stub_withholding` (the stub's, or null when none is known), `entries_outcome` (the result's `projected_refund_cents` in words: `a refund of $0.02`, `a balance due of $3,918.70`, or `no refund and nothing due`), `pay_stub_outcome` (rule 7b's refund or balance due at the stub's withholding in the same words, or null) and `draft` (`RateClaimDraft`: the final row's `concept`, `text` and `what_ifs`). The verdict is `reason` (written first), `claims_entries_in_effect` and `sentence`, the first sentence that makes the claim, required exactly when the claim is made. A present-tense statement is a claim when it describes the withholding the entries give, or the projection with them, as what payroll withholds now or what the year comes to if nothing changes: by naming the entries' amount as what the employer or payroll withholds now ("Your employer withholds $423.16 ... from each paycheck"), or by naming an outcome that only the entries' withholding gives ("withholding is almost exactly matched" when the stub's withholding leaves a balance due). It is not one when it is about the pay stub's withholding or its outcome, when the entries (or their amount) are its subject and it says what they withhold or lead to ("These entries withhold $423.16 a paycheck, against the $272.44 your pay stub shows"), or when it is framed as a choice, a condition or the future ("with these entries", "would", "once your employer has the form"). The judge reports the claim, not its truth: when the stub withholds what the entries give the claim is true, and `withholding_framing` does not score those decisions. It is a second judge, not more of rule 24a's, because the question is about amounts and refund outcomes rather than rates and the full match; one prompt for both would mix their examples. Its request and verdict mirror rule 24a's and reuse `RateClaimDraft` for the draft's words. Its front matter names the `explain` purpose (rule 24a gives why). `FakeGateway` answers "no claim"; an `LLMError` counts the decision or item as missed, with a failure line, and the run goes on.
24e. **Withholding calibration items** (`evals/fixtures/explain/withholding_claims.json`): each has an `id`, the cents a decision would give the judge (`entries_cents`, `pay_stub_cents` or null, `entries_refund_cents`, `pay_stub_refund_cents` or null; the suite formats them as rule 24d says), the draft's `text` (and optionally `concept` and `what_ifs`), the label `claims` and, when it is a sentence from a live explanation, the fixture `decision` it was written for (whose figures it must carry, a test checks). Synthetic only, at least 12 that make no claim and 8 that do, covering the entries' amount named as withheld now, an outcome only the entries give, no stub, a stub that withholds what the entries give, conditionals and "would", the stub's withholding as what is withheld now, the entries as the subject, and a claim in the concept or a what-if. Every row of the withholding framing table below is an item with the same figures and label (a test checks).
25. **`explain-smoke`** (free, `make eval`): the same flow with `ScriptedGateway` (a `FakeGateway` that answers from a script, in `llm/gateway.py`) over eight scenarios: verified on the first try, a failing draft then a verified one, and two failing drafts on a 401(k) decision; the same pass and fail-twice on a W-4 decision (spec 014 rule 19); on issue 132's decision (5% chosen, the stub at 3%), a first try that states the stub's rate and verifies; and on issue 181's decision (the stub withholding $272.44, the entries $423.16), a first try that states the stub's withholding and verifies, and two failing drafts whose fallback states it, verifies and is cited. Metric `flow_correct`: the share of scenarios whose rows (count, `verified`, `fallback`, `attempt`, `retries`) match the expected run. Baseline 1.0.

## Edge cases

- A decision whose `tax_year` has no seeded citations: the allowed list is empty, every draft fails `has_citation`, and the fallback carries no citations. The seed ships 2026 only; a new tax year needs its passages before its decisions can be explained with citations.
- A number the user typed (salary) is allowed because inputs are allowed; a number the model derived (a monthly figure) is not, by design.
- No confirmed pay stub (rule 7a): the context has no stub fact, the note says what goes in now is not known, and `rate_framing` counts the decision. The 401(k) card needs a confirmed stub before it can save, so in practice this is an eval or a test.
- A stub with no retirement line shows 0.00 (spec 005 rule 24b): a person who chose 0% is then contributing it, and `rate_framing` leaves the decision out.
- The two rates are compared by value at two places: a stub at 3.99 against a chosen 4 differs, and the explanation says the chosen rate is not in effect yet. Spec 015's check-in compares cents with a one-cent tolerance instead, because it judges whether payroll applied the election; the explanation only states both rates.
- No confirmed pay stub on the W-4 decision's pay schedule (rule 7b): the context has no stub fact and the note says what is withheld now is not known, and `withholding_framing` counts the decision. The W-4 card needs a confirmed stub to calculate (spec 014 rule 10a), so in practice this is a stub on another schedule, an eval or a test.
- A W-4 stub that withholds nothing is a confirmed amount (spec 014 rule 10a): 0 is compared like any other, so it is `OTHER` unless the entries withhold nothing too (credits larger than the tax, spec 014 example 6), which is `ENTRIES`.
- A W-4 decision saved with `current_withholding_per_paycheck_cents` (spec 014 rule 17a): its stored rule 10a figures stay allowed numbers (rule 12 walks the whole decision), but the context states what the newest stub withholds when the explanation runs, which may differ.
- A model that echoes a citation passage's figures (for example "$8,000" catch-up for a 30-year-old) fails verification unless the figure is also a result or input value; the prompt tells it not to.

## Out of scope

- Explaining check-in findings (`finding_id`, M8).
- Caching across users or reusing an explanation after the decision's version changes: each version is explained on its own.
