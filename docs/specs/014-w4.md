# 014: W-4 withholding calculator

Status: Implemented, M7. Every example is a test and an eval case; every 2026 figure was verified against the IRS documents on 2026-10-21, so no example is pending verification. Rule 10a (the refund or balance due at the newest stub's withholding, examples 11 to 15, rule 17a) implemented for issue 153 on 2026-10-30. Rule 19 changed for issue 181 on 2026-10-30: the explanation tells what the newest stub withholds now from what the entries would withhold (spec 008 rule 7b), with rule 10a's arithmetic shared through `at_withholding`.

## Goal

Show a new salaried employee what their Form W-4 entries will withhold from each paycheck, what federal income tax they will owe for the year, and the refund or balance due that follows, set that beside the refund or balance due if their withholding stays at what their newest confirmed pay stub shows, and suggest the Step 4(c) or Step 4(b) entry that brings the projection within $100 of zero. Withholding follows the IRS Publication 15-T percentage method for automated payroll systems (Worksheet 1A); the projected tax follows the tax year's brackets and standard deduction. The calculator is a pure function in `api/calc/w4.py`, served by `POST /calc/w4`; it saves nothing. `POST /decisions` with `calculator = w4` saves the entries the user picks and adds a reminder to hand the form to the employer.

The 401(k) deferral percentage and the health premium per paycheck come from the user's saved 401(k) and health-plan decisions, and the federal withholding per paycheck from the newest confirmed pay stub; the client reads them and passes them in. `calc/` never reads a decision or a pay stub.

## Rules

Notation: `S` salary, `P` pay periods, `c` the 401(k) rate, `h` the premium per paycheck, `cr` Step 3, `a` Step 4(a), `b` Step 4(b), `x` Step 4(c), `O` and `OW` the other jobs' wages and withholding, `w0` the current withholding per paycheck. Intermediate values are exact `Decimal`; each output is rounded once, half up, to whole cents. Pub 15-T lets an employer round withholding to whole dollars; the calculator keeps cents because payroll systems differ, so a pay stub can show up to 50 cents more or less per paycheck.

1. **401(k) deferral.** `L` is the deferral limit of spec 006 rule 1 for `age` (no catch-up when `age` is null). `E = min(S * c / 100, L)`, `k401_capped = S * c / 100 > L`. The deferral is level across the year, as in spec 006 rule 4. `k401_annual_cents = round(E)`.
2. **Taxable wages.** `W = S - E - h * P`, the year's wages subject to income tax withholding (Worksheet 1A line 1c). The pre-tax 401(k) deferral and the health premium are not wages for income tax withholding. `taxable_wages_per_paycheck_cents = round(W / P)` (line 1a). The worksheet works from `W` itself, not from the rounded line 1a.
3. **Adjusted annual wage** (Worksheet 1A lines 1d to 1i). `g = 0` when `multiple_jobs`, else $12,900 for `married_filing_jointly` and $8,600 otherwise (line 1g, from `pub15t_<tax_year>.json`). `AAW = max(0, W + a - (b + g))`.
4. **Tentative annual withholding** (lines 2a to 2g). The schedule is `step2_checkbox` when `multiple_jobs`, else `standard`, for `filing_status`. The row is the one whose column A is at most `AAW` and whose next row's column A is above it ("at least A but less than B", so `AAW` equal to a row's B takes the next row). `T = C + D / 100 * (AAW - A)`. `withholding_rate_pct = D`.
5. **Per paycheck** (lines 2h to 4b). `line_3c = max(0, T / P - cr / P)`; `withholding_per_paycheck_cents = round(line_3c + x)`.
6. **Annual withholding** is `withholding_per_paycheck_cents * P`: what the paychecks actually withhold, so the rounding of rule 5 shows up in the projection.
7. **Brackets.** `tax(t)` is the progressive tax on `t` from `api/calc/data/<tax_year>.json` (spec 006's table): each bracket's rate on the part of `t` inside it. `tax(t) = 0` for `t <= 0`.
8. **Projected taxable income.** `X = expected_deductions_cents` when set, else `b`. `TI = max(0, W + O + a - standard_deduction - X)`. `projected_taxable_income_cents = round(TI)`. The household's other wages count here because the return taxes them together; they never enter this job's withholding.
9. **Credits.** `projected_tax_cents = round(tax(TI))`; `projected_tax_after_credits_cents = round(max(0, tax(TI) - cr))`. Only the nonrefundable use of the credit is modeled (Out of scope).
10. **Refund or balance due.** `projected_refund_cents = annual_withholding_cents + OW - projected_tax_after_credits_cents`, computed from those rounded outputs so the three add up exactly.
10a. **At current withholding.** When `w0` is set, `current_annual_withholding_cents = w0 * P` and `current_refund_cents = current_annual_withholding_cents + OW - projected_tax_after_credits_cents`; when it is null, both are null. This is what happens if the person hands their employer nothing new.
    - **The tax** is rule 9's `projected_tax_after_credits_cents`, from the entries on screen and the saved 401(k) and health-plan decisions. What the return owes follows from the year's income, deferrals, premiums, other income, expected deductions and credits; it does not depend on which Form W-4 payroll holds. With one tax, the two refunds differ only by withholding: `projected_refund_cents - current_refund_cents = (withholding_per_paycheck_cents - w0) * P`, exactly. The tax at the stub's own deductions would be the wrong year: the stub can show the 401(k) rate and premium from before the saved decisions take effect (stub-01 shows 3%, the e2e account decided 5%).
    - **The year.** A confirmed pay stub holds one paycheck: this period's gross, each deduction and net (spec 005 rule 2). Year-to-date lines are `info` line items and are not stored, so there is nothing to add the paychecks so far from. The comparison therefore assumes every paycheck of the year withholds what the newest stub does, and the card says so. It is a projection of "if nothing changes", not a record of what was withheld so far; a person who started mid-year, or whose withholding changed earlier in the year, will see a different figure on the return. This matches the Out of scope note that the projection assumes the entries apply to every paycheck of the year.
    - **No stub, or a zero.** Confirm requires `federal_withholding_cents` (spec 005 rule 20) and the person types 0 when the stub shows no federal line, so a stored 0 is a confirmed amount and the comparison runs with it: the whole tax, less other jobs' withholding, is due. Only "no figure to compare" is null: no confirmed stub, or a stub whose `pay_frequency` is set and differs from the pay schedule the card uses (its paycheck is a different length, so `w0 * P` would mean nothing). The card cannot calculate at all without a confirmed stub (it reads the tax year and the pay schedule from one) and says so; with a stub for another schedule it says why there is no comparison.
    - **Rounding.** Pub 15-T lets payroll round withholding to whole dollars (the note above), so a stub whose Form W-4 holds exactly the entries on screen can differ from `withholding_per_paycheck_cents` by up to 50 cents a paycheck, `50 * P` cents a year.
    - **The suggestion** (rules 11 to 14) is about the entries on screen and never reads `w0`. The comparison says why handing in the entries matters; the suggestion says what to enter. Once payroll applies the entries, the next stub withholds `withholding_per_paycheck_cents` (within the rounding above), and `current_refund_cents` meets `projected_refund_cents`. Nothing counts the gap from the stub: entering `(withholding_per_paycheck_cents - w0)` in Step 4(c) on top of entries that already close it would withhold the gap twice, so the card never offers it. It states the stub's figure as what happens if nothing changes, then the entries' projection as what handing them in does.
11. **Target.** When `|projected_refund_cents| <= 10,000` ($100) nothing changes: `suggestion = none`, the suggested entries equal the inputs and `suggested_refund_cents = projected_refund_cents`.
12. **Step 4(c) in whole dollars.** For a whole-dollar Step 4(c) entry `n * 100`, the refund becomes `R(n) = projected_refund_cents + (n * 100 - x) * P`, because Step 4(c) is added after every floor of rule 5. The suggested `n` is the non-negative integer that makes `|R(n)|` smallest; a tie goes to the refund (the larger `R(n)`). It is one of `floor(m)` and `ceil(m)`, each raised to 0 if negative, with `m = (x - projected_refund_cents / P) / 100`.
    - A balance due over $100 always takes this route: `suggestion = increase_extra_withholding`.
    - A refund over $100 with `x > 0` takes it first: when `|R(n)| <= 10,000`, `suggestion = decrease_extra_withholding`; otherwise `n` is 0 and rule 13 continues from `x = 0`.
13. **Step 4(b) in whole dollars.** A refund still over $100 with no Step 4(c) left to lower is cut by adding `d` whole dollars to the Step 4(b) entry: `R_b(d)` reruns rules 3 to 6 and 10 with Step 4(b) `b + 100 * d` and the Step 4(c) entry from rule 12, while the projected tax stays as rules 8 and 9 computed it. The Step 4(b) entry then holds more than the deductions the user expects; that is the adjustment the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator also makes, and the user records the expected amount in `expected_deductions_cents` so a saved decision keeps projecting correctly. `R_b` is non-increasing in `d`. With `d_max = ceil(max(0, W + a - b - g) / 100)` (where `AAW` reaches 0) and `floor = max(0, R_b(d_max))`, `d` is the smallest value with `R_b(d) <= floor` (a binary search); when `R_b(d) < 0` and `|R_b(d - 1)| <= |R_b(d)|`, it is `d - 1`. `suggestion = increase_deductions` when `d > 0`.
14. **Suggested entries.** `suggested_extra_withholding_cents` is `n * 100` from rule 12, or `x` when rule 12 did not run; `suggested_deductions_cents = b + 100 * d` (`d = 0` unless rule 13 ran); `suggested_refund_cents` is the refund with both. `suggestion` names the change: `increase_deductions` when `d > 0`, else `increase_extra_withholding` or `decrease_extra_withholding` when the Step 4(c) entry changed, else `none`. A refund that only other jobs' withholding causes may stay over $100 with `suggestion = none`, because nothing on this form can lower it.
15. **Citations.** `citation_keys` is `w4.percentage_method`, `w4.form_adjustments`, `w4.standard_deduction`, `w4.federal_brackets`, in that order, plus `w4.multiple_jobs` when `multiple_jobs` or `O > 0`, plus `w4.dependents_credit` when `cr > 0`, plus `w4.pretax_deferral` when `E > 0`. The passages are in `api/db/seed/irs_citations/w4.yaml`.
16. **Purity and validation.** As spec 006 rules 12 and 13.

### Properties (Hypothesis, `tests/unit/calc/test_w4_properties.py`)

- Every money output is an `int` (or null, only the two of rule 10a); every money output except the three refunds is non-negative.
- `withholding_per_paycheck_cents` is non-decreasing in `salary_cents`, with everything else fixed.
- `annual_withholding_cents == withholding_per_paycheck_cents * pay_periods_per_year` and `projected_refund_cents == annual_withholding_cents + other_jobs_withholding_cents - projected_tax_after_credits_cents`.
- `increase_extra_withholding` always lands within $100: `|suggested_refund_cents| <= 10,000`. `decrease_extra_withholding` does too, unless the suggested Step 4(c) is 0 and the refund that is left (from other jobs' withholding) is one rule 13 cannot lower.
- `|projected_refund_cents| <= 10,000` implies `suggestion = none`, and `suggestion = none` implies `suggested_refund_cents = projected_refund_cents`.
- Entering the suggested Step 4(b) and 4(c) entries, with `expected_deductions_cents` kept at the expected deductions, projects `suggested_refund_cents`.
- `current_annual_withholding_cents` and `current_refund_cents` are null exactly when `current_withholding_per_paycheck_cents` is; otherwise `current_annual_withholding_cents == w0 * P`, `current_refund_cents == current_annual_withholding_cents + other_jobs_withholding_cents - projected_tax_after_credits_cents`, and `projected_refund_cents - current_refund_cents == (withholding_per_paycheck_cents - w0) * P`.
- `current_withholding_per_paycheck_cents` changes nothing else: every other output is the same with it null.
- `at_withholding(data, w4(data), w0)` gives `w4(data with w0)`'s `current_annual_withholding_cents` and `current_refund_cents` for every `w0`.
- The function is pure: two calls with equal inputs return equal outputs.

### Saving a decision

17. Saving a `w4` decision follows spec 007 (validate, recompute, compare, store the server's values).
17a. `current_withholding_per_paycheck_cents` is stored with the inputs like every input, so the saved result's rule 10a figures can be recomputed. It describes payroll today, not an entry on the form: the W-4 card compares a saved decision with the inputs on screen without it, so a newer stub alone (such as the first one after the employer applies the form) does not mark a saved decision changed. Decisions saved before rule 10a have no such input and recompute with null; their stored results have no rule 10a fields, which read as null (the two result fields default to null so those rows still validate).
18. The save adds one calendar event: kind `w4_submit`, title "Give your employer your updated Form W-4", due the next expected pay date (spec 007 rule 16), linked to the decision. A later version dismisses the earlier version's `upcoming` `w4_submit` events in the same transaction. `DecisionSaved.calendar_event_id` is the new event's id; `goal_id` is null. Migration `0009_w4_calendar_kind` (revising `0011_no_model_reason`) adds the `calendar_kind` label `w4_submit`.
19. **Explain.** The plan states the decision's own projection. The explanation states it as what the entries would withhold and lead to, and beside it what the newest confirmed stub on the decision's pay schedule withholds now and rule 10a's refund or balance due at that withholding, read when the explanation runs and never from the decision's own `current_withholding_per_paycheck_cents` (spec 008 rule 7b, issue 181). Those figures come from `calc/w4.py:at_withholding(data, result, w0)`, which `w4()` also uses for its rule 10a fields: `(w0 * P, w0 * P + OW - projected_tax_after_credits_cents)` from the stored result, so a saved decision is never recomputed to explain it. `retrieval/context.py` turns a W-4 decision into facts, each a number (the withholding these entries would give per paycheck and per year, what the latest stub withholds when known and, when it differs, the year's withholding and the refund or balance due at it, the projected tax before and after credits, the refund or balance due, the taxable wages per paycheck, the adjusted annual wage, the schedule rate, the 401(k) deferral and premiums for the year, the Step 3 and Step 4 entries that are set, the other jobs' wages and withholding when set, the suggested entries and the refund with them, the salary, paychecks per year and tax year), and notes in words (whether the stub withholds what the entries give, another amount or nothing is known; refund or balance due with the entries and, when the stub differs, at the stub; the Step 2 box, the suggestion, a capped 401(k) deferral). The benefits query is `pre-tax premium payroll deduction 401(k)`. `verify/fallback.py` writes the code-only fallback from the same numbers, cited with the result's `citation_keys` the seed holds. Spec 008's examples for rule 7b work this on example 11 (stub-01 at $272.44 against the entries' $423.16, $3,918.70 due at the stub).

### The Pub 15-T example

Pub 15-T (2026) works no percentage-method example: its only worked example is a nonresident alien's 2019 Form W-4 on the wage bracket tables, which this calculator does not model. Example 1 is therefore Worksheet 1A worked line by line for a wage that is the midpoint of a row of the publication's own Wage Bracket Method table (section 2, biweekly, single), and the result agrees with that printed table entry once rounded to the dollar, the way the wage bracket tables are built.

## Edge cases

- **Premiums and deferral above the salary.** `health_premium_per_paycheck_cents * P + E > S` is a validation error (422).
- **A row boundary.** `AAW` equal to a row's column B takes the next row: single, 26 periods, salary $66,500.00 gives `AAW = 57,900`, the 22% row, `T = 5,800`. Test `test_adjusted_wage_on_a_row_boundary`.
- **Catch-up.** Example 10 at age 52 and 13%: the requested `25,000,000 * 13% = 3,250,000` equals `L = 2,450,000 + 800,000`, so `k401_capped = false` and `E = 3,250,000`; `AAW = 217,500 - 8,600 = 208,900`, the 24% row, `T = 17,966 + 24% * 95,700 = 40,934`, 157,438 a paycheck (`test_catch_up_raises_the_deferral_limit`).
- **Step 2 checked with no other job** over-withholds: single, $65,000.00 biweekly, `AAW = 65,000` in the checkbox 24% row, `T = 8,983 + 24% * 4,100 = 9,967`, 38,335 a paycheck, refund `996,710 - 562,000 = 434,710`. Rule 13 suggests Step 4(b) $19,387.00 (1,938,700), refund -10 (`test_step2_without_other_job_suggests_deductions`). Unchecking Step 2 is the better fix and the explanation's note says so.
- **`expected_deductions_cents`.** Saving example 9's suggestion as `deductions_cents = 1,068,300` with `expected_deductions_cents = 0` projects a refund of -10 and suggests nothing (`test_saved_deductions_adjustment_projects_the_same_tax`).
- **Other jobs' withholding alone over-withholds.** When this job withholds nothing and `OW` exceeds the tax, the refund stays over $100 with `suggestion = none`: single, $10,000.00 here (`AAW = 1,400`, the 0% row), other jobs $20,000.00 with 500,000 withheld, tax on `30,000 - 16,100 = 13,900` is 142,000, refund 358,000 (`test_refund_from_other_jobs_has_no_suggestion`).
- **Zero wages after the deferral and premiums** give `AAW = 0` (or Step 4(a) alone), no withholding beyond Step 4(c), and a projection that still counts other income.
- **A tax year without constants** (either file) is a 422 naming the year.
- **A stub for another pay schedule.** The profile says semimonthly and the newest stub biweekly: the card sends `current_withholding_per_paycheck_cents` null and says the stub's paycheck is for a different schedule. A stub with no `pay_frequency` is taken to be on the card's schedule, the one the card already reads its pay periods from when the profile has none.
- **An old decision.** A W-4 decision saved before rule 10a has no `current_withholding_per_paycheck_cents`; it recomputes as null, and its stored result reads with both rule 10a fields null (`test_result_without_the_comparison_still_reads`).

## Out of scope

- The Step 2(b) Multiple Jobs Worksheet and its tables: the user's other jobs come in as wages and withholding, and the suggestion reaches the same result through Step 4(c).
- The refundable part of the child tax credit, the earned income credit and other refundable credits; Social Security and Medicare taxes; state income tax; the Additional Medicare Tax and the net investment income tax.
- Exemption from withholding, nonresident alien adjustments, supplemental wages (bonuses) and Forms W-4 from 2019 or earlier.
- A mid-year change: the projection assumes the entries apply to every paycheck of the year, and rule 10a assumes every paycheck withholds what the newest stub does. Year-to-date withholding is not stored (spec 005), so neither projection counts the paychecks already paid.
- Roth 401(k) deferrals, which do not lower taxable wages.
