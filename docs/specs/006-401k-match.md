# 006: 401(k) match calculator

Status: Implemented, M3. Every example is a test. The 401(a)(17) compensation limit applies to the match (rule 3a, decided for M3); open question 2 remains. Rule 14 (where the card's contribution starts, and how its headline speaks) and example 11 added for issue 132.

## Goal

Tell the user which contribution percentage captures the employer's full 401(k) match, how many dollars of match they get at their current percentage and how many they leave on the table, and what moving to the full-match percentage costs per paycheck before and after federal income tax. The calculator is a pure function in `api/calc/k401.py`, served by `POST /calc/401k-match`. It saves nothing; `POST /decisions` saves the result the user picks.

## Inputs

| Field | Type | Meaning and validation |
|---|---|---|
| `salary_cents` | `Cents` (int) | Annual base salary. Must be > 0. |
| `pay_periods_per_year` | int | One of 12, 24, 26, 52. |
| `match_tiers` | list of `{employee_pct_upto: Decimal, employer_pct: Decimal}` | The employer formula, from `profiles.match_tiers`. `employee_pct_upto` is the cumulative upper bound of the tier in percent of salary; tiers are strictly ascending by it, each in (0, 100]. `employer_pct` is the share of that tier the employer matches, in (0, 100]. May be empty (no match). |
| `contribution_pct` | Decimal | The pre-tax deferral rate the employee is choosing, in percent of salary, in [0, 100], at most 2 decimal places. `4.5` means 4.5%. It is the rate being considered or saved, not necessarily the one payroll applies now: that one is the pay stub's `retirement_contribution_pct` (spec 005 rule 24b, rule 14 below). The outputs below call it "current" because it is the rate on screen. |
| `tax_year` | int | Derived from the pay stub's `pay_date` by the caller (spec amendment 13). Must have a constants file `api/calc/data/<tax_year>.json`, else 422. |
| `filing_status` | enum | `single`, `married_filing_jointly`, `head_of_household` (the W-4 Step 1(c) groups). Selects the bracket table and standard deduction. |
| `age` | int | Age the user reaches by December 31 of `tax_year`. Selects the catch-up amount. Non-negative. |

Percentages are `Decimal` in percent points throughout, never floats.

## Outputs

| Field | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `full_match_pct` | Decimal | Lowest contribution percentage that earns the whole match: the last tier's `employee_pct_upto`, or 0 with no tiers. |
| `employer_match_cents_current` | `Cents` | Annual employer match at `contribution_pct`. |
| `employer_match_cents_full` | `Cents` | Annual employer match at `full_match_pct`. |
| `missed_cents` | `Cents` | `employer_match_cents_full - employer_match_cents_current`. Never negative. |
| `employee_annual_cents_current` | `Cents` | Annual employee deferral at `contribution_pct`, after the deferral limit. |
| `employee_annual_cents_full` | `Cents` | Annual employee deferral at `full_match_pct`, after the deferral limit. |
| `per_paycheck_cost_delta_cents` | `Cents` | Extra deferral per paycheck to move from the current to the full-match percentage. 0 when already at or above it. |
| `per_paycheck_after_tax_cost_cents` | `Cents` | The same move's drop in take-home pay per paycheck after federal income tax savings at `marginal_rate_used`. |
| `marginal_rate_used` | Decimal | Federal ordinary-income rate, in percent points, of the bracket that holds the user's estimated taxable income at the current contribution. |
| `deferral_limit_cents` | `Cents` | 402(g) limit for `tax_year` plus the catch-up amount for `age`. |
| `capped` | bool | True when `contribution_pct` of salary exceeds `deferral_limit_cents`. |
| `compensation_capped` | bool | True when `salary_cents` exceeds the 401(a)(17) compensation limit for `tax_year`, so the match is computed on the limit instead of the salary (rule 3a). |
| `citation_keys` | list of str | Keys into `irs_citations` (calculator `k401`, same `tax_year`) the explanation may cite. |

## Rules

Notation: `S = salary_cents`, `c = contribution_pct`, `F = full_match_pct`, `P = pay_periods_per_year`, `L = deferral_limit_cents`. Intermediate values are exact `Decimal`; only outputs are rounded, each once, with `round_half_up` to whole cents.

1. **Deferral limit.** `L = limit_402g + catch_up`, where `catch_up` is 0 for `age < 50`, `catch_up_50` for `age >= 50` outside 60-63, and `catch_up_60_63` for `60 <= age <= 63`. The three amounts come from `api/calc/data/<tax_year>.json`.
2. **Full-match percentage.** `F = max(employee_pct_upto)` over the tiers, or 0 when there are none.
3. **Employee deferral, exact.** `E(p) = min(S * p / 100, L)` for a percentage `p`. `E_cur = E(c)` and `E_full = E(F)`.
3a. **Compensation limit for the match.** `S_m = min(S, compensation_limit)`, where `compensation_limit` is the 401(a)(17) annual compensation limit from `api/calc/data/<tax_year>.json` (2026: $360,000, 36,000,000 cents). `compensation_capped = S > compensation_limit`. Only the match uses `S_m` (rule 5); the deferral (rule 3), the cap (rule 4) and taxable income (rule 9) use `S`. `F` stays the last tier's bound (rule 2): a plan that matches each paycheck needs the rate `F` on every paycheck to earn the whole match, even though this annual model reaches it at a lower rate once `S_m < S`.
4. **Capped.** `capped = S * c / 100 > L`. The calculator models a level rate across every paycheck, so a capped user is treated as deferring exactly `L` over the year. It does not model front-loading (see Edge cases).
5. **Employer match, exact.** With tier bounds `B_0 = 0` and `B_i = S_m * employee_pct_upto_i / 100`, `M(E) = sum over i of (employer_pct_i / 100) * max(0, min(E, B_i) - B_(i-1))`.
6. **Match outputs.** `employer_match_cents_current = round(M(E_cur))`, `employer_match_cents_full = round(M(E_full))`, `missed_cents = employer_match_cents_full - employer_match_cents_current`. Because `M` is non-decreasing and constant above `B_last`, `missed_cents >= 0`.
7. **Employee outputs.** `employee_annual_cents_current = round(E_cur)`, `employee_annual_cents_full = round(E_full)`.
8. **Per-paycheck cost.** `D = max(0, E_full - E_cur)`. `per_paycheck_cost_delta_cents = round(D / P)`.
9. **Marginal rate.** Estimated taxable income `T = S - E_cur - standard_deduction(tax_year, filing_status)`. If `T <= 0` the rate is 0. Otherwise it is the rate of the bracket whose range holds `T`, where a bracket's upper bound is inclusive ("not over"), so `T` exactly on a boundary takes the lower rate. `marginal_rate_used` is that rate in percent points.
10. **After-tax cost.** `per_paycheck_after_tax_cost_cents = round(D * (1 - marginal_rate_used / 100) / P)`. Only federal income tax is modeled: 401(k) deferrals do not reduce Social Security or Medicare withholding, and state tax is out of scope (spec section 3, Tax scope).
11. **Citations.** `citation_keys` is always `k401.employer_match`, `k401.pretax_deferral`, `k401.deferral_limit` and `k401.federal_brackets`, in that order, plus `k401.catch_up` when `age >= 50`, plus `k401.compensation_limit` when `compensation_capped`.
12. **Purity.** No I/O, no clock, no randomness; the same input gives the same output. Imports only stdlib, `decimal`, `pydantic` and `calc.*` (enforced by `tests/unit/test_boundaries.py`).
13. **Validation.** Any input outside the ranges in Inputs is a 422 from the route and a `ValidationError` from the Pydantic model.
14. **Where the card's contribution starts, and how it speaks (client, issue 132).** The 401(k) card on `/setup/decisions/401k` (spec 018; `web/src/components/screens/decisions/Match401kCard.tsx`) starts its contribution from, in order: the current saved decision's `contribution_pct`; else the newest confirmed pay stub's `retirement_contribution_pct` (spec 005 rule 24b), the rate payroll applies now; else 0. The person moves it from there. The calculator is unchanged: it is sent whatever rate is on screen. The result's headline speaks in the present ("You leave $950 of match behind", "You capture the full $3,800 match", "You leave the whole $3,800 match behind") only when the rate on screen is the rate the newest stub shows, compared as the two-place strings the API sends (`"3.00"`); for any other rate, or when the stub has no rate, it speaks of that rate as a choice ("At 5% you would capture the full $3,800 match", "At 0% you would leave the whole $3,800 match behind"). So the card never tells a person who contributes that they leave the whole match behind. The card's intro names the stub's rate beside its dollars ("shows $109.62 a paycheck, 3% of your gross pay, going to your 401(k)"); without a rate it names the dollars only. The client compares the two rates' strings and never works one out.

### Properties (Hypothesis, `calc/test_k401_properties.py`)

- Every money output is an `int`.
- `0 <= employer_match_cents_current <= employer_match_cents_full`.
- `employer_match_cents_current` is non-decreasing in `contribution_pct` with everything else fixed.
- If `c >= F`, `missed_cents == 0` (then `E_cur >= E_full`, and `M` is non-decreasing).
- If neither limit binds at `F` (`S * F / 100 <= L` and `compensation_capped` is false) and `c < F`, then `missed_cents > 0` whenever the exact difference is at least one cent, which holds when `min(employer_pct) / 100 * S * (F - c) / 100 >= 1`. When a limit binds, `missed_cents` can be 0 below `F` (example 9, and any `c` just under `F` that is already capped). This corrects the plan's "missed is zero iff contribution >= full-match pct", which fails when either limit binds below `F` and which rounding to whole cents could break for differences under a cent.
- `0 <= per_paycheck_after_tax_cost_cents <= per_paycheck_cost_delta_cents`.
- `employee_annual_cents_current <= deferral_limit_cents`.
- The function is pure: two calls with equal inputs return equal outputs.

### Arithmetic

**1. Tiered match, 100% of the first 3% plus 50% of the next 2%.** $60,000.00 salary, biweekly, contributing 3%. This tier shape is also the safe harbor basic matching formula of IRC 401(k)(12)(B), so this example doubles as the plan's safe harbor case.
- `F = 5`. `B_1 = 6,000,000 * 3% = 180,000`; `B_2 = 6,000,000 * 5% = 300,000`.
- `E_cur = 6,000,000 * 3% = 180,000` (below `L`). Match: `1.00 * 180,000 + 0.50 * max(0, 180,000 - 180,000) = 180,000`.
- `E_full = 300,000`. Match: `180,000 + 0.50 * (300,000 - 180,000) = 180,000 + 60,000 = 240,000`. Missed `240,000 - 180,000 = 60,000`.
- `D = 300,000 - 180,000 = 120,000`; per paycheck `120,000 / 26 = 4,615.38...`, rounds to 4,615 ($46.15).
- `T = 6,000,000 - 180,000 - 1,610,000 = 4,210,000` ($42,100), which is over $12,400 and not over $50,400: 12%.
- After tax: `120,000 * 0.88 / 26 = 105,600 / 26 = 4,061.53...`, rounds to 4,062 ($40.62).

**2. Simple 100% match up to 4%.** The mockup's sample: $65,000.00 salary, semi-monthly (gross $2,708.33 per paycheck), contributing 2%.
- `F = 4`. `E_cur = 6,500,000 * 2% = 130,000`; match `130,000`.
- `E_full = 6,500,000 * 4% = 260,000`; match `260,000`. Missed `130,000` ($1,300 a year).
- `D = 130,000`; per paycheck `130,000 / 24 = 5,416.66...`, rounds to 5,417.
- `T = 6,500,000 - 130,000 - 1,610,000 = 4,760,000` ($47,600): 12%.
- After tax: `130,000 * 0.88 / 24 = 114,400 / 24 = 4,766.66...`, rounds to 4,767.

**3. 50% match up to 6%.** $85,000.00 salary, biweekly, contributing 4%.
- `F = 6`. `E_cur = 8,500,000 * 4% = 340,000`; match `0.50 * 340,000 = 170,000`.
- `E_full = 8,500,000 * 6% = 510,000`; match `0.50 * 510,000 = 255,000`. Missed `85,000`.
- `D = 170,000`; per paycheck `170,000 / 26 = 6,538.46...`, rounds to 6,538.
- `T = 8,500,000 - 340,000 - 1,610,000 = 6,550,000` ($65,500), over $50,400 and not over $105,700: 22%.
- After tax: `170,000 * 0.78 / 26 = 132,600 / 26 = 5,100` exactly.

**4. The 2026 402(g) limit binds.** $300,000.00 salary, biweekly, tiered match, contributing 10%, age 30.
- `L = 2,450,000` (Notice 2025-67). Requested `30,000,000 * 10% = 3,000,000 > 2,450,000`, so `capped` and `E_cur = 2,450,000`.
- `B_1 = 900,000`, `B_2 = 1,500,000`. Match: `900,000 + 0.50 * (min(2,450,000, 1,500,000) - 900,000) = 900,000 + 300,000 = 1,200,000`.
- `E_full = 1,500,000` (below `L`); match `1,200,000`. Missed 0.
- `D = max(0, 1,500,000 - 2,450,000) = 0`, so both per-paycheck costs are 0.
- `T = 30,000,000 - 2,450,000 - 1,610,000 = 25,940,000` ($259,400), over $256,225 and not over $640,600: 35%.

**5a and 5b. 24 versus 26 pay periods.** $52,000.00 salary, tiered match, contributing 2%. Annual results are identical; only per-paycheck values differ.
- `F = 5`. `E_cur = 104,000`; match `104,000` (under `B_1 = 156,000`).
- `E_full = 260,000`; match `156,000 + 0.50 * (260,000 - 156,000) = 156,000 + 52,000 = 208,000`. Missed `104,000`.
- `D = 156,000`. Biweekly: `156,000 / 26 = 6,000`. Semi-monthly: `156,000 / 24 = 6,500`.
- `T = 5,200,000 - 104,000 - 1,610,000 = 3,486,000` ($34,860): 12%.
- After tax: `156,000 * 0.88 = 137,280`; `/ 26 = 5,280`; `/ 24 = 5,720`.

**6. Contribution above the full-match percentage.** $70,000.00 salary, biweekly, 100% up to 4%, contributing 8%.
- `F = 4`. `E_cur = 560,000`; match `min(560,000, 280,000) = 280,000`.
- `E_full = 280,000`; match `280,000`. Missed 0.
- `D = max(0, 280,000 - 560,000) = 0`; both per-paycheck costs 0. `employee_annual_cents_full` (280,000) is below `employee_annual_cents_current` (560,000), which is expected: the full-match figure is the minimum that earns the whole match.
- `T = 7,000,000 - 560,000 - 1,610,000 = 4,830,000` ($48,300): 12%.

**7a. Catch-up at age 52.** Example 4 at age 52.
- `L = 2,450,000 + 800,000 = 3,250,000`. Requested 3,000,000 is under it: not capped, `E_cur = 3,000,000`.
- Match and missed as in example 4 (the match tops out at `B_2`).
- `T = 30,000,000 - 3,000,000 - 1,610,000 = 25,390,000` ($253,900), over $201,775 and not over $256,225: 32%. The larger deferral moves the user down a bracket.

**7b. Catch-up at age 61.** Example 4 at age 61, contributing 12%.
- `L = 2,450,000 + 1,125,000 = 3,575,000`. Requested `30,000,000 * 12% = 3,600,000 > 3,575,000`: capped, `E_cur = 3,575,000`.
- `T = 30,000,000 - 3,575,000 - 1,610,000 = 24,815,000` ($248,150): 32%.

**8. Fractional cents round half up once, at the end.** $45,123.45 salary, biweekly, tiered match, contributing 4.5%.
- `E_cur = 4,512,345 * 4.5% = 203,055.525`, reported as 203,056.
- `B_1 = 4,512,345 * 3% = 135,370.35`; `B_2 = 4,512,345 * 5% = 225,617.25`.
- Current match: `135,370.35 + 0.50 * (203,055.525 - 135,370.35) = 135,370.35 + 33,842.5875 = 169,212.9375`, reported as 169,213.
- Full match: `135,370.35 + 0.50 * (225,617.25 - 135,370.35) = 135,370.35 + 45,123.45 = 180,493.80`, reported as 180,494. Missed `180,494 - 169,213 = 11,281`.
- `E_full = 225,617.25`, reported as 225,617.
- `D = 225,617.25 - 203,055.525 = 22,561.725`; per paycheck `22,561.725 / 26 = 867.758...`, rounds to 868.
- `T = 4,512,345 - 203,055.525 - 1,610,000 = 2,699,289.475` ($26,992.89): 12%.
- After tax: `22,561.725 * 0.88 / 26 = 19,854.318 / 26 = 763.627...`, rounds to 764. Rounding `D / P` first and then applying the rate would give `868 * 0.88 = 763.84`, also 764 here, but rule 10 computes from the exact `D`.

**9. Both limits bind below the full-match percentage.** The plan's "$500,000 salary at 6% sets capped" case.
- `S = 50,000,000 > 36,000,000`, so `compensation_capped` and `S_m = 36,000,000` (rule 3a, Notice 2025-67).
- Requested `50,000,000 * 6% = 3,000,000 > 2,450,000`: capped, `E_cur = 2,450,000` (4.9% of salary).
- `E_full = min(50,000,000 * 5%, 2,450,000) = min(2,500,000, 2,450,000) = 2,450,000`.
- `B_1 = 36,000,000 * 3% = 1,080,000`; `B_2 = 36,000,000 * 5% = 1,800,000`. Match: `1,080,000 + 0.50 * (min(2,450,000, 1,800,000) - 1,080,000) = 1,080,000 + 360,000 = 1,440,000` for both, which is `$360,000 * (3% + 0.5 * 2%) = $14,400`. Without rule 3a it would have been 1,975,000 ($19,750). Missed 0; `D = 0`, so both per-paycheck costs are 0.
- `T = 50,000,000 - 2,450,000 - 1,610,000 = 45,940,000` ($459,400), over $256,225 and not over $640,600: 35%.
- Citation keys: rule 11's four plus `k401.compensation_limit`.

**10. Zero contribution.** Example 1 with `c = 0`.
- `E_cur = 0`, match 0. `E_full = 300,000`, match 240,000. Missed 240,000.
- `D = 300,000`; per paycheck `300,000 / 26 = 11,538.46...`, rounds to 11,538.
- `T = 6,000,000 - 0 - 1,610,000 = 4,390,000` ($43,900): 12%.
- After tax: `300,000 * 0.88 / 26 = 264,000 / 26 = 10,153.84...`, rounds to 10,154.

**11. The stub of issue 132, at the rate it shows.** The synthetic `stub-01` pays $3,653.85 biweekly, so the salary it implies is `365,385 * 26 = 9,500,010` cents ($95,000.10, spec 005 rule 24a), and it shows $109.62 a paycheck going to the 401(k), a rate of 3.00% (spec 005 rule 24b, row 1). The benefits summary matches 100% up to 3% and 50% up to 5%. With no saved decision, the card starts at 3% (rule 14), so this is what it shows first.
- `F = 5`. `B_1 = 9,500,010 * 3% = 285,000.30`; `B_2 = 9,500,010 * 5% = 475,000.50`.
- `E_cur = 285,000.30`, reported as 285,000. Match: `1.00 * 285,000.30 = 285,000.30`, reported as 285,000 ($2,850).
- `E_full = 475,000.50`, a tie on the half cent, reported as 475,001 (half up). Match: `285,000.30 + 0.50 * (475,000.50 - 285,000.30) = 285,000.30 + 95,000.10 = 380,000.40`, reported as 380,000 ($3,800). Missed `380,000 - 285,000 = 95,000` ($950), not the whole $3,800 the card claimed when it started at 0%.
- `D = 475,000.50 - 285,000.30 = 190,000.20`; per paycheck `190,000.20 / 26 = 7,307.70`, rounds to 7,308.
- `T = 9,500,010 - 285,000.30 - 1,610,000 = 7,605,009.70` ($76,050.10), over $50,400 and not over $105,700: 22%.
- After tax: `190,000.20 * 0.78 / 26 = 148,200.156 / 26 = 5,700.006...`, rounds to 5,700.
- The headline (rule 14): at 3%, the stub's rate, "You leave $950 of match behind". Moved to 5% and saved: "At 5% you would capture the full $3,800 match", because the stub still shows 3% until payroll applies the new election; once a stub shows 5.00, "You capture the full $3,800 match". Moved to 0%: "At 0% you would leave the whole $3,800 match behind".

The examples were worked by hand and then cross-checked with a throwaway `Decimal` script before this spec was committed; the tests, not the script, are the record.

## Edge cases

- **No match.** Empty `match_tiers` gives `F = 0`, both match outputs 0, `missed_cents = 0`, `employee_annual_cents_full = 0`, and both per-paycheck costs 0.
- **Taxable income on a bracket boundary** takes the lower rate ("not over"). Test `test_bracket_boundary_takes_lower_rate`: single, `S = 6,650,000`, `c = 0`, so `T = 6,650,000 - 0 - 1,610,000 = 5,040,000` ($50,400, the top of the 12% bracket); expects `marginal_rate_used = 12`. With `S = 6,650,001` it expects 22.
- **Taxable income at or below zero** gives `marginal_rate_used = 0` and an after-tax cost equal to the pre-tax cost.
- **Bracket crossing.** When moving from `E_cur` to `E_full` crosses a bracket boundary, the calculator still applies the single rate at `E_cur`. The difference is at most the rate gap times the part of `D` in the lower bracket, and the output names the rate it used so the explanation can say so.
- **Front-loading.** A capped user who keeps a high rate stops deferring once they reach `L` and, unless the plan has a true-up, gets no match on the remaining paychecks. The calculator assumes a level rate; `capped = true` is the signal for the explanation to warn about this.
- **Catch-up eligibility** assumes the plan allows catch-up contributions. The Roth catch-up requirement for prior-year FICA wages over $150,000 (Notice 2025-67) does not change the limit and is not modeled.
- **Tax year without constants** (for example 2027 before its file exists) is a 422 naming the missing year, never a silent fallback to another year.
- **Non-integer contribution percentages** up to two decimals are allowed; example 8 covers rounding.
- **Where the card starts (rule 14).** A saved decision wins over the stub: a person who saved 5% sees 5% even while the stub still shows 3%, and the headline speaks of 5% as a choice. A stub with no retirement line has a rate of 0.00, so the card starts at 0% and, since that is the stub's rate, says "You leave the whole ... match behind", which is then true. A stub whose rate is null (a zero gross, which confirm refuses) leaves the card at 0%, speaking conditionally. A rate that is no whole percentage (3.69 for a $100.00 flat election on a $2,708.33 paycheck) is where the card starts, shown as "3.69% of salary"; the slider moves in whole steps from there.
- **Salary exactly at the compensation limit** is not capped. Test `test_compensation_limit_boundary`: single, tiers 3@100 and 5@50, `c = 5`, `S = 36,000,000` gives `compensation_capped = false` and a match of 1,440,000; `S = 36,000,001` gives `compensation_capped = true` and the same match, because `S_m` stays 36,000,000.

## Out of scope

- State income tax, Social Security and Medicare effects of the deferral.
- Roth 401(k) deferrals (after-tax), after-tax non-Roth contributions, and the 415(c) annual additions limit ($72,000 for 2026).
- Vesting. `profiles.vesting_years` is stored (spec amendment 15) but this calculator does not discount unvested match.
- True-up provisions and per-paycheck match timing.
- Pre-tax deductions other than the 401(k) deferral (health premiums, HSA) in the taxable-income estimate.

## Open questions

1. **401(a)(17) compensation limit.** Decided for M3: the match uses compensation up to the limit (rule 3a), `compensation_limit_cents` is in `2026.json`, and the result reports `compensation_capped`.
2. **Where `age` comes from.** Settled in M6: `profiles.birth_year` (nullable `smallint`, migration `0008_goals_and_birth_year`, `CHECK (birth_year >= 1900)`; `PUT /profile` also refuses a year after the request's current year) holds it, returned by `GET /profile`. The calculator still takes `age` as an input; the client sends `age = tax_year - birth_year`, the age reached by December 31 of the tax year, so `calc/` never reads the profile.
3. **Seed citations.** Decided for M3: `api/db/seed/irs_citations/k401.yaml` holds every key rule 11 can emit (`k401.employer_match`, `k401.pretax_deferral`, `k401.deferral_limit`, `k401.federal_brackets`, `k401.catch_up`, `k401.compensation_limit`) plus `k401.vesting`, each verified against the live IRS page (spec 008).
